# Ultimate Guide to Finding No-Fee Apartments in NYC (2024-2025)

Searching for an apartment in New York City can be overwhelming, especially when broker fees can cost you 12-15% of your annual rent or more. That's where no-fee apartments come in—a way to save thousands of dollars on your move.

## What Are No-Fee Apartments?

No-fee apartments are rentals where the landlord or building management pays the broker's commission instead of the tenant. In a city where broker fees typically range from one to two months' rent, finding a no-fee apartment can save you anywhere from $2,000 to $6,000 or more.

### The Real Cost of Broker Fees in NYC

Let's break down the math:
- **Average NYC 1BR rent:** $3,500/month
- **Typical broker fee:** 12-15% of annual rent
- **Total broker fee:** $5,040 - $6,300

That's a significant chunk of money you could put toward furniture, moving costs, or simply keep in your savings account.

# Frequently Asked Questions About No-Fee Apartments in NYC

## General Questions About No-Fee Apartments

### What exactly is a "no-fee" apartment?
A no-fee apartment is a rental where the landlord or building management pays the broker's commission instead of the tenant. In a typical broker-fee rental, tenants pay 12-15% of the annual rent (often one month's rent or more) directly to the broker. With no-fee apartments, you avoid this cost entirely.

### How much can I save with a no-fee apartment?
Savings vary based on the rent amount, but typically you'll save one to two months' rent. For example:
- On a $3,000/month apartment: Save $3,000-$6,000
- On a $4,000/month apartment: Save $4,000-$8,000
- On a $5,000/month apartment: Save $5,000-$10,000

### Are no-fee apartments lower quality than broker-fee apartments?
No! No-fee apartments are simply properties where the landlord has chosen to pay the broker commission (or rent directly without a broker). You'll find no-fee options across all price ranges and quality levels, from budget-friendly studios to luxury penthouses.

### Why do some landlords pay the broker fee while others don't?
Landlords typically pay broker fees for several reasons:
- They own multiple properties and use a management company
- The building has its own leasing office
- They want to attract more qualified tenants
- The rental market is slower and they need to incentivize renters
- They're managing the property themselves without a broker

### How do I find legitimate no-fee apartments?
- Use specialized no-fee sites like NoFeesApts.com
- Look for "For Rent By Owner" (FRBO) listings
- Contact building management companies directly
- Check with large residential complexes
- Be wary of listings marked "CYOF" (Collect Your Own Fee)—these often still charge broker fees

### What is "CYOF" and should I avoid it?
CYOF stands for "Collect Your Own Fee." While it may appear in no-fee search results, it actually means the broker expects YOU to pay their fee. Always ask directly: "Who pays the broker fee?" before scheduling a viewing.

### Do I still need to pay application fees for no-fee apartments?
Yes. No-fee refers specifically to the broker fee. You'll still typically pay:
- Application fee: $20-$100 per applicant
- Credit check fee (often included in application fee)
- First month's rent
- Security deposit (usually 1 month's rent)
- Move-in fees (if applicable)

Standard application requirements include:

### What are the income requirements for NYC apartments?
Most NYC landlords require:

### How long are typical NYC apartment leases?
Most NYC leases are 12 months, but you may find:
- 6-month leases (often at a premium)
- 9-month leases (less common)
- 18-24 month leases (sometimes offered with incentives)
- Month-to-month (rare and usually more expensive)

### What's included in my rent?
This varies by building, but typically:
- **Usually included**: Water, heat (during winter)

- **Rarely included**: Electricity, internet, cable
- **Always ask** what utilities are included before signing

### How much is a security deposit in NYC?
- Typically one month's rent for buildings with 6+ units
- May be higher for luxury buildings or if you have pets
- Must be held in an interest-bearing account (buildings with 6+ units)
- Must be returned within 14 days of move-out with itemized deductions

### What happens if I need to break my lease?
Breaking a lease early typically involves:

- Possible early termination fee (usually 1-2 months' rent)

### Can I sublet my NYC apartment?
New York State law gives tenants the right to sublet with landlord's permission:
- Must get written permission from landlord
- Landlord can refuse for valid reasons
- Typically allowed for up to 2 years
- You remain responsible for the apartment
- Check your lease—some buildings have stricter rules

- May-September: Peak moving season
- End of month: Everyone wants the same move-in dates
- Back-to-school season: August-September for college students

## Building Amenities & Features

### What amenities should I prioritize?
Consider your lifestyle:
- **Must-haves for most**: In-unit laundry or building laundry, elevator (for 4+ floor walkups)
- **Nice to have**: Doorman, gym, roof deck, package room
- **Luxury features**: Concierge, parking, swimming pool, co-working spaces
- Remember: More amenities usually mean higher rent and possible amenity fees

### Do doorman buildings cost more?
Yes, doorman buildings typically cost 10-20% more than non-doorman buildings, but they offer:
- Package acceptance
- Building security
- Guest screening
- Maintenance coordination
- Often better maintained buildings

### What's the difference between a studio, alcove studio, and 1-bedroom?
- **Studio**: One open room serving as bedroom and living room, plus bathroom and kitchen
- **Alcove Studio**: Studio with a small alcove (nook) that can fit a bed or be used as a separate space
- **Junior 1-Bedroom**: A studio with a separate sleeping area that's partially enclosed but not a full separate room
- **1-Bedroom**: Completely separate bedroom with a door, plus living room

### Are pets allowed in NYC apartments?
Pet policies vary by building:
- Some buildings are pet-friendly with no restrictions
- Some allow cats but not dogs
- Some allow dogs under a certain weight (typically 25-50 lbs)
- Pet deposits: $200-$500 one-time or $25-$100/month
- Always disclose pets—hiding a pet can result in eviction

## Payment & Financial Questions

### What forms of payment do landlords accept?
For move-in costs:
- Certified check or bank check (most common)
- Wire transfer (some buildings)
- Money order (less common)
- Personal check (rarely accepted for first payment)

For monthly rent:
- Personal check
- Automatic bank transfer (ACH)
- Payment portal (increasingly common)
- Never cash

### Do I need renter's insurance?
Most landlords require renter's insurance:
- Covers your belongings in case of theft, fire, water damage
- Provides liability coverage
- Costs $15-$30/month for $30K-$50K coverage
- Much cheaper than replacing your belongings

### What if I don't have good credit?
Options if your credit score is below 650:
- Use a guarantor (family member with good credit and 80x income)
- Use a third-party guarantor service (Insurent, TheGuarantors)
- Offer to pay several months upfront
- Provide additional references
- Be prepared to pay a higher security deposit

### Can I use a guarantor if I don't meet income requirements?
Yes. Guarantors typically need:
- Annual income of 80x the monthly rent
- Good credit score (usually 700+)
- US-based (for most landlords)
- Willing to sign legally binding guarantee
- Alternative: Third-party guarantor services for a fee

## Legal & Rights Questions

### What are my rights as a tenant in NYC?
NYC tenants have strong legal protections:
- Right to a habitable apartment (heat, hot water, no pests)
- Right to a signed, written lease
- Right to privacy (landlord must give 24-hour notice to enter)
- Right to return of security deposit (minus legal deductions)
- Protection against discrimination
- Right to organize with other tenants

### Can my landlord raise my rent whenever they want?
No. Rent increases are regulated:
- **Rent-stabilized apartments**: Increases set by Rent Guidelines Board (typically 1-3% annually)
- **Market-rate apartments**: Landlord can only raise rent when lease renews
- **Required notice**: 30 days for month-to-month, per lease terms for annual leases
- **Good Cause Eviction Law (2024)**: Limits increases in certain buildings

### What can I do if my landlord won't make repairs?
Steps to take:
1. Request repairs in writing (email or certified letter)
2. Give reasonable time for response (30 days for non-emergency)
3. Document the issue with photos/videos
4. File a complaint with NYC HPD (311)
5. Consider withholding rent in escrow (consult lawyer first)
6. Contact a tenant advocacy group

### Can my landlord evict me?
Landlords can only evict for legal reasons:
- Non-payment of rent
- Lease violation
- Owner or family member moving in (in some cases)
- Building renovation or demolition
- Illegal activity
- **Process**: Must go through housing court—landlord cannot simply lock you out

### What happens to my security deposit?
- Landlord has 14 days after move-out to return deposit (buildings with 6+ units)
- Must provide itemized list of any deductions
- Legal deductions: Unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear and tear
- Not legal: Normal wear and tear, painting, cleaning (unless excessive)
- Take move-in and move-out photos to protect yourself
